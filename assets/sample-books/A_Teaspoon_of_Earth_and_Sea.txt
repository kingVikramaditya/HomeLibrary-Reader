A TEASPOON OF EARTH AND SEA
By Dina Nayeri

CHAPTER I: The Shores of Gilan

Growing up in the northern province of Gilan by the Caspian Sea, Saba Hafezi knew that life was divided into two halves: the world that existed before the revolution, and the world that came after. Her twin sister, Mahtab, had vanished into the turquoise waves when they were eleven years old, or so everyone in their small seaside town whispered.

Yet Saba clung to an unshakeable belief. She imagined another version of their lives unfolding across the ocean in America, where girls rode bicycles with ribbons in their hair, ate cherries in tall glass sundaes, and read books under the warm sun without fear.

The autumn breeze carried the salt of the Caspian and the aroma of saffron rice drifting from neighboring stone kitchens. Saba sat on the wooden veranda, clutching her notebook of English vocabulary words. Each word was a ticket to an imagined world: "Horizon," "Sanctuary," "Serendipity."

CHAPTER II: The Memory of Water

Every story Saba told herself had two endings. In one, her mother had boarded an airplane heading west, holding Mahtab by the hand. In the other, the dark current of the sea had swallowed their secrets whole. Her father, a quiet man who tended his tea plantation with weathered hands, rarely spoke of that fateful July afternoon.

"Memory is not a museum, Saba," her father would say, stirring black tea with a cardamom pod. "It is living soil. What you plant inside it will either nourish you or drown your roots."

CHAPTER III: Letters Across the Sea

By the winter of 1989, Saba had accumulated seventy-two letters addressed to a foreign address she had memorized from an old postcard found in an antique wooden bureau. She wrote of the mountain snow caps, the sweet taste of pomegranate paste, and the melodies her music teacher played on the tar.

She dreamed of stepping off an airplane into a bustling terminal filled with yellow cabs, tall glass skyscrapers, and wide avenues where no one asked who you were or what memories you carried.
